# Vision pillars

Part of [Vision and pillars](vision.md): its Game pillars and Product pillars sections.

## Game pillars

Listed in rank order. When two pillars conflict, the higher rank wins. Pillars judge a proposal when rules are silent; every other page cites them by ID.

### GP-1 Same wiring, same result

- Rank: 1 among game pillars. Over GP-2: a report never changes between runs of one wiring, even when a new joke would be funnier. Over GP-3: an agent's rule reads plainly on its face, even where mission jargon would sound drier. Over GP-4: no wiring wins on some runs and fails on others, even if that would open more ways to win.
- Means: nothing in play is random. Each agent shows its rule on its face. One wiring gives one run and one report, every time.
- Not: random events; hidden agent rules; timing or reflex skill.
- Review check: can the player explain this result from what the wiring board shows?
- Playtest check: after a run, testers explain what came out; asked before a run, they predict it more often as an act goes on.

### GP-2 Every failure is a joke and a clue

- Rank: 2 among game pillars. Over GP-3: a report names the agent at fault plainly, even when that breaks the formal voice. Over GP-4: an order narrows its wirings when an open wiring board would leave its failures generic.
- Means: every wrong run ends in an incident report built from the items' history, from authored lines for each agent and item kind, plus hand-written results for each order's likeliest wrong wirings. The report names the agents each item passed and where its marks first differ from the required ones, so the funny result also points to the wrong wire. A repair order's incident report names only its first failing item. Trying again costs nothing: a run fast-forwards or skips to its report, and a report already filed prints at once. Each order shows how many of its hand-written results the player has found. Collecting reports never gates progress. The campaign opens, at the player's first tap, on the night staff's wiring of order 1 misfiring; a tap skips it.
- Not: a bare "wrong output"; a gag unrelated to the wiring; a cost per failed run, such as lives or runs counted in the score.
- Review check: what does each likely wrong wiring of this order produce, and which wire does its report point to?
- Playtest check: testers wire wrong on purpose to read the report; after one report, testers name the wire to change.

### GP-3 Mission Control, played straight

- Rank: 3 among game pillars. Over GP-4: an order may limit its solutions to keep its stakes straight.
- Means: every order arrives as a humanity-critical mission, in dry procedural language: an order in at most 20 words, an agent's rule in at most 5, a report led by one result line. The comedy comes from literal agents obeying exactly, and shows on the items. Reports are formal records of absurd results. The screen is a 1970s space-agency control console: flat Bauhaus shapes, few colours, labelled jacks and switches, teletype printouts. Each agent family has its own silhouette and one indicator lamp: off when idle, steady while working, plus or minus on a verdict. The master alarm is the board's one red lamp. Data wires are solid; feedback wires are dashed and marked plus or minus; wires run straight from jack to jack, and a crossing shows a small hop. Lit marks blink on the jacks a held wire can plug into. The first feedback wire comes in an order that opens with it already patched. Sounds are relays, switches, tones and teletype.
- Not: winking jokes; puns aimed at the player; the game laughing at itself; slapstick sounds; cartoon mascots, glossy gradients, a modern app look.
- Review check: would a 1970s mission controller say this text, and would this frame fit on their console?
- Playtest check: testers quote an order or a report; asked what the game looks like, testers name a 1970s space control room.

### GP-4 Many right wirings

- Rank: 4 among game pillars.
- Means: any wiring whose output matches the required output wins. Par makes a second, leaner wiring worth finding. Repair orders accept any fix.
- Not: one intended solution, except in orders of one or two agents; a win that depends on which wiring the player chose.
- Review check: does this order have at least two distinct wirings that win? Exception: orders of one or two agents, which allow few wirings.
- Playtest check: two testers win the same order with different wirings; testers replay an order to beat par.

## Product pillars

Listed in rank order, below every game pillar.

### PP-1 Plays anywhere from a link

- Rank: 1 among product pillars.
- Means: every feature works within Platform and Constraints. A link opens the first order. A report can leave the game: the player copies it as text with the game's link, or saves it as a picture of the drawn result at the output, its result line, and the game's link printed and as a QR code. Where the device has a share sheet, it opens too. With Product goals, it judges help and pacing proposals: a player who opens the link should reach the end of act 1.
- Not: online features beyond the counts, such as leaderboards or shared solutions; an install or sign-in step.
- Review check: does this work within Platform and Constraints, opened from a link?
- Playtest check: a tester opens the link on a phone in portrait and wins the first order with no help; another wins an order of the last act on a phone.
